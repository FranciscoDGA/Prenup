Short answer: **between $600 and $15,000** — and where you land has almost nothing to do with love, and almost everything to do with state, complexity, and how many rounds of back-and-forth your lawyers bill.

Here are the real 2026 numbers, where they come from, and the four line items that make couples' eyes go wide.

## The three routes (and what each costs)

| Route | Typical 2026 cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Online service** (flat fee) | **$600 – $1,500** | Straightforward finances: one house, one business-less couple, similar incomes |
| **Online + attorney review** | **$1,200 – $2,500** | The sweet spot for most couples — flat-fee document, real lawyer checking it |
| **Attorney-drafted, uncontested** | **$1,500 – $4,000** | Moderate complexity: property, kids from prior marriages, income gap |
| **Attorney-drafted, contested** | **$5,000 – $15,000+** | Businesses, multiple properties, stock comp, or partners who can't agree |

Attorneys bill hourly — **$300–$500/hour** in most major metros, $400–$600+ in New York, San Francisco, and Boston. A "simple" attorney-drafted prenup often takes 5–15 hours across drafting, review, negotiation, and execution. That's where the range comes from.

## The four line items that blow up the bill

### 1. Each partner hiring their own attorney

Sometimes required, sometimes just wise — but yes, it roughly doubles legal fees. It's also the single strongest insurance that the agreement survives: courts scrutinize whether both sides had *real* opportunity for independent advice.

### 2. The negotiation rounds nobody quotes

The first draft is cheap. The three rounds of "change clause 7, and while you're at it..." at $350/hour are not. **Agree on the big terms before the lawyer starts typing** — separate property, debt, what happens to the house — and most couples cut hours off the bill.

### 3. Complex assets

A business, rental properties in two states, RSUs that vest over four years, a family trust — each one adds valuation work, custom clauses, and review time. Complex estates don't just cost more to *write*; they cost more to make *enforceable*.

### 4. Waiting until the wedding month

Rush fees are real, leverage is gone, and mistakes are expensive. Couples who start 3–6 months out routinely pay less than couples who start 3 weeks out — same document, fewer panicked billable hours.

## How to keep a prenup cheap without cutting corners

1. **Start early.** Three to six months before the wedding is the sweet spot.
2. **Do your disclosure homework first.** Gather statements, deeds, and account lists before the first appointment.
3. **Agree on terms together** — then pay the lawyer to *document* the agreement, not to discover it.
4. **Consider the online route** if your finances are straightforward, and spend the savings on an hour of attorney review instead of full drafting.
5. **Don't DIY the execution.** Notarization, witnesses, and state formalities are where cheap documents die.
