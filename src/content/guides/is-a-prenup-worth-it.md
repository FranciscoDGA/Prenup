Let's drop the romance for ninety seconds and talk about this like the purchase it is.

You're about to spend an average of **$33,000 on a wedding**. Someone in the comments will say prenups are unromantic. Meanwhile the prenup costs less than the floral arrangements — and unlike the floral arrangements, it still matters in ten years.

Here's the actual math.

## The cost of a prenup vs. the cost of not having one

| What you're comparing | Typical cost (US) |
|---|---|
| Online prenup service (flat fee) | **$600 – $1,500** |
| Attorney-drafted, straightforward | **$1,500 – $4,000** |
| Attorney-drafted, complex (business, real estate, stock) | **$5,000 – $15,000+** |
| **Contested divorce, per person** | **$15,000 – $30,000+** |
| Uncontested divorce *with* an existing prenup | **$1,000 – $5,000** |

Read that table twice. The most expensive prenup on the list costs about the same as one party's share of a mid-range contested divorce — and the prenup is the *bad case* scenario for the document itself.

The insurance analogy isn't cute, it's accurate: you don't buy car insurance because you plan to crash. You buy it because the crash, if it comes, arrives at the worst possible moment — and you'd rather have decided the terms in February than argued them in November.

## What a prenup actually buys

Three things, in order of value:

### 1. You keep the pen

Without a prenup, your state's legislature already wrote your divorce terms — sight unseen, for a generic couple, decades ago. Community-property states default to 50/50 of marital assets. Equitable-distribution states hand a judge a wide discretion and let them call it "fair."

Neither is evil. Both are *average*. Your marriage isn't average, and the assets in it aren't average either.

A prenup replaces "what a stranger decides is fair" with "what we decided together, while we still agreed on everything."

### 2. You cut the bill if it ends

Divorce lawyers bill hourly — **$300–$500 an hour** in most metros — and the fights that eat those hours are almost always *property* fights: who gets the house, whose business is worth what, whether the pre-retirement account grew with marital money.

A prenup settles those questions in advance. Custody and support still go to court no matter what — but the expensive, emotional property war is off the table before it starts.

### 3. You protect people who aren't in the room

If you have kids from a previous marriage, a prenup is less about your spouse and more about your children's inheritance. If you expect a family business or trust, it's about the relatives who built it. These are the cases where "not worth it" turns into permanent regret.

## When a prenup is genuinely *not* worth it

Honesty matters more than conversion here. Skip it if:

- **You're both truly starting at zero.** No house, no savings, no inheritance, similar earnings, no kids from prior relationships. A prenup would only protect future earnings — a values call, not a math call.
- **One partner will refuse to sign fairly.** A prenup forced under deadline pressure is a prenup a court may throw out later — meaning you paid for a false sense of security.
- **Your assets are simple and your state's defaults already match your values.** Some couples look at their state's default rules and shrug: *that's what we'd choose anyway.* Fine. Document that conversation somewhere and move on.

## The number that actually matters

Ask one question: **if we divorced in three years, what would we each walk away with — and would we both think that's fair?**

If the answer is obvious and mutual, you may not need much. If the answer makes either of you hesitate — because the house is in one name, the business is in another, or the earnings gap is wide — then a prenup isn't a bet against the marriage.

It's the cheapest fight you'll ever avoid.
